How to shoot so the colour and cut-out work
  • Put it down on one plain surface. The app measures the very edge of the photo to find the background — if a desk, a floor and a wall all meet there, it gives up. A sheet of paper, a door or a bedsheet is enough.
  • Leave a clear margin all round. Nothing but that surface should touch the frame edge — no hands, no table edge, no skirting.
  • Do not hold it. A hand in the shot becomes part of the piece, and skin skews the colour it reads.
  • Contrast matters. Dark pieces on a pale surface, pale pieces on a dark one. Black on a near-black rug reads as charcoal, and a white shirt on white paper vanishes into it.
  • Fill about half the frame. Too small and it is mistaken for clutter; too large and there is no background left to remove.
  • Even, indirect light. Daylight from a window is ideal. No flash and no hard sun — blown highlights and black shadows carry no colour, so they are thrown away.
  • Warm bulbs are corrected, up to a point. A very orange lamp is left alone rather than half-fixed, so colours will read warm. Shoot by a window if you can.
  • One piece per photo, shot straight on, whole thing in frame.
